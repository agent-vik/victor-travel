杭州萧山机场(12:50) - 曼谷素万那普机场(16:25)

  • 值机

中午起飞（有飞机餐），傍晚落地，不安排游玩。

D2

（2月xx日）湄南河沿线

景点：大皇宫&玉佛寺(2小时) - 卧佛寺(2小时) - 唐人街夜市(1小时)

  • 大皇宫&玉佛寺
  • 卧佛寺

地铁Silom line到Sala Daeng站，换Blue line到Sanam Chai站。步行去参观大皇宫和玉佛寺。然后步行游览旁边卧佛寺。回Sanam Chai站，乘地铁到Wat Mangkon站，逛唐人街夜市。最后打车回酒店。

  • 普吉岛接机

小提示

  • 曼谷和普吉岛所在时区比北京时间晚1小时。
  • 泰国汽车靠左行驶，注意交通安全。
  • 泰国皇宫寺庙有着装要求，不可露出肩膀、膝盖，要穿长裤。
  • 泰国很多室内公共场所和交通工具空调开得非常冷，需要带外套。
  • 海上自驾皮划艇有一定危险，带小孩最好不玩。
  • 泰国有藏蓝色PromptPay标识的二维码都支持微信支付。
  • 在泰国换汇，优先找Super Rich绿色门店，汇率和国内接近。
  • 泰国ATM取现一般固定收220铢手续费，尽量减少取款次数。
  • 泰国ATM取现，要拒绝货币转换（Accept without currency conversion），使用银联的汇率更优惠。
  • ATM取款时，尝试往外拔插卡口，松动就大概率是侧录器。
  • 泰国的高端大额消费，商品价格可能显示为“金额++”形式，1个加号代表商家服务费（10%），另一个代表增值税（7%），实际付款金额大约会是显示金额的1.2倍。有商家服务费就不用再给小费了。
  • 泰国有小费文化，但并非美国那种强制式社会规范，服务超出预期才给：
    1. 20铢纸币是最常用的小费选项，一般不拿硬币作为小费，因为金额小冒犯对方。
    2. 按摩店小费建议给50-100铢，这是技师的重要收入来源。
    3. 酒店行李员可以给20-50铢。
    4. 客房清洁如果每天留20铢在床头柜，能换来更细致的清洁和服务。
    5. 包车司机和导游可以给100-300铢。
    6. 出租车、便利店、连锁快餐、路边摊、普通餐厅不用给。
  • 香港出租车目前还需要用现金。
  • 香港地铁禁止饮食。
  • 香港叮叮车后门上车，前面下车，下车时付款。
  • 香港仅有少数场合给小费：
    1. 酒店行李员可以给20-50港币。
    2. 客房清洁可以在退房当天留20-50港币在床头柜。
  • 带药出国要有药盒药瓶，不然被安检当违禁品扯不清。
  • 泰国贵重物品存放策略：
    1. 所有证件、历史机票、备用银行卡放行李箱，上锁。
    2. 手机、主力银行卡放在胸包。
    3. 等值￥500应急现金，藏在一包纸巾里放口袋（被抢劫还能应急打车）。
    4. 约2日开销的现金放在胸包。
    5. 其余现金放行李箱，上锁。
    6. 乘机或换酒店当天，把护照、港澳通行证和现金暂时转移到胸包。

Tips

  • Bangkok and Phuket are 1 hour behind Beijing time.
  • Thailand drives on the left — watch traffic carefully.
  • Thai palaces and temples have a dress code: cover shoulders and knees; wear long pants.
  • Many indoor public spaces and transit in Thailand run AC very cold — bring a light jacket.
  • Self-paddled kayaks at sea have real risk; better skip with young kids.
  • In Thailand, QR codes with the dark-blue PromptPay mark accept WeChat Pay.
  • For currency exchange in Thailand, prefer Super Rich green-door shops — rates close to mainland China.
  • Thai ATMs usually charge a flat ฿220 fee — minimize withdrawals.
  • At Thai ATMs, decline currency conversion (Accept without currency conversion); UnionPay rates are better.
  • When withdrawing, try gently pulling the card slot outward — if it feels loose, it’s likely a skimmer.
  • For upscale Thai purchases, prices may show as “amount++”: one + is service charge (10%), the other VAT (7%); you pay roughly 1.2× the listed price. If there’s a service charge, no need to tip on top.
  • Thailand has a tipping culture, but it’s not a hard US-style social rule — tip when service exceeds expectations:
    1. A ฿20 note is the usual tip; don’t tip with coins — the amount is small and can feel insulting.
    2. Massage tip ฿50–100 — an important part of the therapist’s income.
    3. Hotel porters: ฿20–50.
    4. Leave ฿20 on the nightstand daily for housekeeping for more attentive cleaning.
    5. Private drivers and guides: ฿100–300.
    6. No tip for taxis, convenience stores, chain fast food, street stalls, or ordinary restaurants.
  • Hong Kong taxis still need cash for now.
  • No eating or drinking on the Hong Kong MTR.
  • Hong Kong trams (ding ding): board at the rear, exit at the front, pay when you get off.
  • Hong Kong tipping is rare — only a few cases:
    1. Hotel porters: HK$20–50.
    2. Housekeeping: leave HK$20–50 on the nightstand on checkout day.
  • Bring medicine abroad in original boxes/bottles, or security may treat it as contraband and argue endlessly.
  • Valuables strategy in Thailand:
    1. All IDs, old boarding passes, and spare bank cards in the locked suitcase.
    2. Phone and primary bank cards in the chest bag.
    3. ~¥500 emergency cash hidden in a tissue pack in a pocket (enough for a taxi if robbed).
    4. About 2 days’ spending cash in the chest bag.
    5. Remaining cash in the locked suitcase.
    6. On flight days or hotel-change days, move passport, Home Return Permit, and cash into the chest bag temporarily.
